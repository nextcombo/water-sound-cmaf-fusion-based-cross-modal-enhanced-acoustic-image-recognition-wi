# [Water Sound] CMAF: Fusion-based Cross-Modal Enhanced Acoustic Image Recognition [with code attached]

Introduction: Underwater image recognition is a crucial yet challenging task in marine exploration and military tactical reconnaissance. Objects in terrestrial environments can be easily classified based on their shape, texture, and contextual information. In contrast, classification tasks in underwater environments are significantly hindered by several factors such as poor lighting, water turbidity, and unpredictable environmental noise or vortices [19,33]. Specifically, underwater image datasets can be categorized into two types based on the method of image capture: optical or acoustic cameras [41]. Most existing works address the classification problem for optical camera datasets due to their rich visual features [23,27]. However, since the attenuation coefficient is proportional to the working frequency [1], light may suffer rapid underwater channel attenuation when passing through water. As a result, the performance of this data is limited to shallow environments and is sensitive to environmental brightness. In contrast, acoustic cameras can capture visual features from longer observation distances without being limited by brightness. However, images captured by acoustic cameras may have poor resolution and degraded target features due to low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) caused by environmental noise [61].
In this paper, we use acoustic images captured by the sonar cameras as input and a fish classification dataset as target datasets. Since fish classification falls under fine-grained classification, it allows for better differentiation of different model performances. To address the problem of fish classification in acoustic images, a hybrid model proposed in [46] uses image processing techniques to analyze image histograms, first identifying the objects of interest, then using machine learning models to classify these objects to overcome the difficulties in analyzing underwater images. In [22], image processing techniques are used to segment objects to deal with noisy acoustic images, and artificial neural networks are employed for object classification. Unlike most existing works that focus on improving the quality of visual features, which often require manual knowledge in the field of feature extraction, our goal is to provide an enhanced sonar signal modality function injection prior knowledge to improve the performance of underwater acoustic image recognition more effectively.
To overcome the above shortcomings, this paper proposes a fusion-based cross-modality enhancement (CMAF) framework. Specifically, we first use pre-trained models to identify targets by finding bounding boxes, which provide environmental information (e.g., depth) and the characteristics of the target. Then, to address the issue of deficiencies in visual features, the processing process is divided into two branches: the visual modality branch and the sonar signal modality branch. The visual modality branch utilizes pre-trained models to extract features from the bounding boxes. Conversely, the sonar signal modality is generated by a sonar emulator, which inputs based on the underwater channel model and manually parameterized as target strength. Moreover, to reduce the difficulty of collecting target strength data, this paper also integrates target strength according to the existing experimental results and geometric information in the image data. The sonar signal modality aims to provide complementary character features rather than only relying on visual information.
To enhance the joint representation between different modalities, we designed an attention-based cross-modal fusion encoder to appropriately fuse different modalities while simultaneously enhancing the correlation between different modalities. Additionally, real-world data often follow a long-tail distribution, where minority classes contain most samples, while other classes have relatively few samples. Classification performance may decrease due to class imbalance issues. To address this imbalance problem, we utilized a mask-based training strategy to enhance learning from local features. A common method is applying focal loss, which is effective in mitigating the imbalance issue. However, focal loss does not consider the mask-based training strategies based on varying degrees of difficulty. Therefore, we proposed a new loss function that alleviates the imbalance issue by modulating the mask with a parameter based on the masked modulation, taking into account the changes in training difficulty and working in conjunction with the training strategy to improve overall performance.
The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follows:
- We proposed a modality enhancement method that produces sonar signals' functionality, providing a different modality feature acoustically based fish image. By utilizing the complementarity of enhanced sonar signal features with the original visual features, we addressed the issue of insufficient information content for sonar-based images.
- We designed an attention-based cross-modal fusion encoder that fuses visual features and sonar signal features to obtain more robust representations of different modality features. The attention mechanism allows the model to focus on the key parts of features through learning the correlation between them. The fused features combine self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms, providing a more robust representation method.
- We adopted a training strategy and designed a mask-based focal loss to match the training strategy to address the data imbalance issue and improve overall performance. This training strategy can enhance the model's ability to learn from local features. By designing hyperparameters in the loss function, we improved the classification performance of difficult classes, avoiding the issue of class imbalance.
- We evaluated the performance of our proposed method and compared it with state-of-the-art classification models. Benefiting from enhanced modalities and design loss functions, we observed improved performance and alleviated class imbalance issues.

Figure 1 illustrates the overall architecture proposed by fusing the CMAF framework across modalities. Given an image, its location is from the entire sonar image, aiming to classify each fish. The proposed architecture is divided into two parts based on visual and sonar signal modalities. To enhance the correlation between these two modalities, a cross-modal fusion module is utilized to fuse features of images and sonar signals. Additionally, to strengthen the learning of local features, we propose a mask-based training strategy that randomly masks features, thereby disrupting the information of global features. This forces the model to rely more on information from local features. Since the dataset is collected from real environments, due to the uneven distribution of samples, classification results may lead to class imbalance issues. A new mask-based focal loss method is proposed to address the issue of data distribution imbalance in the dataset.

Simulation Results
The conclusion of this paper mainly introduces the CMAF framework. In response to the issue of insufficient acoustic image information, a modality enhancement method based on visual modality information is proposed to enhance the modality of sonar signals. On this basis, an attention-based encoder is designed to fuse visual features and sonar signal features. Additionally, we have adopted a mask-based training strategy to improve overall performance. A mask-based focal loss algorithm is proposed to complement the training strategy to reduce class imbalance issues. Experiments show that compared to traditional methods, the proposed algorithm has higher clustering accuracy; in the future, due to the poor resolution of acoustic images, we plan to enhance image quality to improve the performance of target intensity generators. We also plan to study the optimal mask ratio for each sample to effectively maximize local feature learning and jointly optimize with hyperparameter settings.
